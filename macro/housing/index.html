Where is the housing cycle?

The housing pipeline in order: permits lead starts, starts lead completions, completions feed inventory, inventory sets pricing power, prices and mortgage rates set affordability, and affordability drives turnover. The score below combines seven of these series; the sections after it show about twenty, one mechanism at a time.

What the score is made of

Which inputs push the score up, which hold it down, and how it moved over three months. A description of the data, not a forecast or a recommendation.

1. Activity pipeline — permits → starts → completions → sales

The cycle flows from permits (intent) to starts (actual breaking-ground) to completions (supply hitting the market) to sales (absorption). Permits lead starts by 1–2 months; completions lag starts by 6–9 months. Single-family and multi-family run different cycles — SF tracks owner-occupier demand and rates; MF tracks rent dynamics + cap rates.

2. Inventory & prices

Months of supply is homes for sale divided by the monthly sales pace. For existing homes the usual reading is below 4 a sellers’ market, 4–6 balanced, above 6 a buyers’ market; FRED carries only the last 13 months of that series (it is NAR data), so the long history shown is the Census new-home series. Prices follow inventory with a lag of a few quarters. The rental vacancy rate measures the rental side: tight rentals feed rent and owners’-equivalent-rent inflation.

3. Affordability — can people actually buy?

Affordability depends on three things: the home price, the mortgage rate and income. The red line is the principal-and-interest payment on a 30-year fixed mortgage for the median new home (20% down) as a share of median household income, both in current dollars. Lenders’ rule of thumb caps housing costs at about 28% of gross income, and that includes taxes and insurance, which this ratio leaves out.

4. Stress, construction & materials

Mortgage delinquency shows whether existing owners are under payment stress; it lags the price cycle by a quarter or two. Construction employment is the labor side of the cycle: builders shed workers when starts slow. Lumber PPI is an input cost for building and remodeling. Residential construction spending measures activity in dollars.

5. Housing-linked equities

One-year price performance of housing-linked equities against the S&P 500: XHB (homebuilders and their supply chain), ITB (a narrower builder fund), WOOD (timber and forestry), the two large home-improvement retailers and one building-materials distributor. Shown as market context, not as a view on any of them.
