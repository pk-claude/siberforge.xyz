How loose or tight are credit conditions?

Reading the data…

Credit conditions carry Fed policy into the real economy: when spreads widen and lenders pull back, borrowing gets dearer for everyone. Six signals: NFCI and adjusted NFCI, Baa and Aaa corporate spreads, the 10Y−3M curve and the 10-year real yield.

1. Financial conditions — NFCI & ANFCI

Chicago Fed's National Financial Conditions Index aggregates 105 measures of money-market stress, credit-market spreads, and leverage. Above 0 = tighter than average; below 0 = looser. The adjusted version (ANFCI) controls for current cycle position, isolating "tightness vs. what the cycle would imply." NFCI is largely coincident: it tightens as stress arrives, so treat readings above zero as confirmation rather than an early warning.

2. Credit spreads

A credit spread is the extra yield investors demand to hold corporate bonds instead of Treasuries: the market's price of default risk. The long history here is Moody's Baa and Aaa yields minus the 10-year Treasury, which is what the composite scores. FRED now carries only about three years of the ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads (high yield and investment grade), so those appear as short dashed lines. When high yield widens much faster than investment grade, stress is concentrated in the weakest borrowers.

3. Yield curve & real yields

The 10Y-3M curve is the NY Fed's preferred recession-model input. Inversion (negative spread) preceded every US recession since 1968 with a lag of roughly 6-18 months, and also inverted from 2022 into 2025 with no recession following. The 10-year real yield (TIPS) is the long-term cost of borrowing after expected inflation; it averaged about 0.4% from 2010 to 2019.

Methodology & sources

Score. Each input is ranked against its own last 20 years, with the direction set so that 100 means the most risk; the score is their weighted average. The inputs, weights and transforms are listed under “How this score is built” next to the score.

Data. Data: FRED. NFCI/ANFCI Chicago Fed; OAS spreads ICE BofA; curve / real yields US Treasury via FRED. Read from a static snapshot refreshed twice each weekday.

Limits. History uses today’s revised figures. BLS published no October 2025 CPI or household-survey data; that month is interpolated so year-over-year rates span 12 months. Thresholds quoted in the tiles are reference points from history, not rules. More on sources and method.
