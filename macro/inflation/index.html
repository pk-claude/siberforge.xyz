Is the current inflation print persistent or transitory?

Reading the data…

The Fed cares about what they can’t undo quickly: services, shelter, wages, and whether long-run expectations are drifting. This page decomposes the monthly print into its durable vs. reversible components. If sticky and shelter and wages are all elevated, the Fed is structurally tight regardless of where headline prints. If only flexible/energy is hot, the situation reverses in one bad oil print. Related: regime view, cycle position.

What the score is made of

Which inputs push the score up, which hold it down, and how it moved over three months. A description of the data, not a forecast or a recommendation.

1. Headline vs Core vs Core PCE

Three measures of the same thing with different baskets and volatility profiles. Headline (with food & energy) is the press-cycle number. Core CPI strips those out for persistence. The Fed’s 2% goal is defined on headline PCE; core PCE is its preferred guide to the underlying trend. PCE uses a broader basket with shifting weights and has usually run a few tenths below CPI. When headline runs above core, food and energy are rising faster than everything else; when it runs below, they are a drag.

2. Sticky vs Flexible — the persistence decomposition

The Atlanta Fed splits the CPI basket by how often prices change. Sticky items (rent, medical care, education, insurance) change price less often than about every four months. Flexible items (used cars, apparel, lodging, motor fuel) change more often. Sticky-price inflation is the most persistent part: it responds slowly even to rate hikes. When flexible prices are cool and sticky prices stay hot, the problem is in services, which has historically taken sustained restrictive policy to bring down.

3. Market-implied expectations — are they anchored?

Breakevens = nominal minus TIPS yield at each tenor. The 5-year breakeven reflects the next 5 years of expected inflation + a risk premium. The 5y5y forward is the market’s view of the average from years 5 through 10 — it strips out the current inflation dynamics, so it’s the cleanest read on whether long-run expectations are anchored near the Fed’s 2% target. UMich’s consumer 1y expectation is the survey comparison — consumer expectations tend to be volatile and reflect grocery prices as much as policy credibility.

4. Wage & shelter pressure — the structural feeders

Wages drive services prices (labor is the largest services cost). Shelter is about 35% of the CPI and 44% of core, and lags market rents by 6–12 months (leases reprice slowly), so once shelter turns it tends to keep going. Real wages = average hourly earnings YoY minus CPI YoY; when they are negative, prices are outpacing pay.

Methodology & sources

Score. Each input is ranked against its own last 20 years, with the direction set so that 100 means the most risk; the score is their weighted average. The inputs, weights and transforms are listed under “How this score is built” next to the score.

Data. Data: FRED (Core PCE, Core CPI, Sticky-Price CPI, Flex-Price CPI, breakevens, CPI shelter, AHE, UMich). Pink bands on time-series = NBER recessions. Read from a static snapshot refreshed twice each weekday.

Limits. History uses today’s revised figures. BLS published no October 2025 CPI or household-survey data; that month is interpolated so year-over-year rates span 12 months. Thresholds quoted in the tiles are reference points from history, not rules. More on sources and method.
