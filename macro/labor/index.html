How strong — or how fast weakening — is employment?

Reading the data…

Employment drives household income and spending, and it turns late in the cycle. Five signals: unemployment level, the Sahm Rule trigger, initial claims, payroll growth, and wage growth.

1. Unemployment & the Sahm Rule

The Sahm rule triggers when the 3-month average unemployment rate rises 0.5 percentage points above its lowest 3-month average of the previous 12 months. It has confirmed every recession since 1970 within a few months of the start; the July 2024 trigger was not followed by one. The line shown is FRED’s real-time series (first-release data).

2. Initial jobless claims (4-week MA)

The most timely labor signal: weekly, with the 4-week average smoothing holiday and weather noise. Claims ran roughly 200–275K through the 2017–19 and 2022–24 expansions and passed 350K around the start of the 2001 and 2008 recessions. Low claims mean few layoffs; they say nothing about hiring, which is why claims can sit near record lows while payroll growth is weak.

3. Payrolls & wage growth

Payroll growth over six months, annualized, is the hiring pulse. Before 2025 a pace under 1% was a recession warning; slower labor-force growth since then has lowered the pace needed to hold unemployment steady, so the same reading means less. Wage growth (average hourly earnings, year over year) gauges bargaining power. When wages rise more slowly than the CPI, real wages are falling.

Methodology & sources

Score. Each input is ranked against its own last 20 years, with the direction set so that 100 means the most risk; the score is their weighted average. The inputs, weights and transforms are listed under “How this score is built” next to the score.

Data. Data: FRED. UNRATE / PAYEMS / CES0500000003 from BLS; IC4WSA from DOL via FRED. Read from a static snapshot refreshed twice each weekday.

Limits. History uses today’s revised figures. BLS published no October 2025 CPI or household-survey data; that month is interpolated so year-over-year rates span 12 months. Thresholds quoted in the tiles are reference points from history, not rules. More on sources and method.
